 The index of physical 
volume (real growth rates)  
of GDP is calculated as the ratio 
of the GDP of the reporting 
period, calculated in constant 
prices, i.e. in prices of the 
corresponding period of the last 
year (real GDP) to GDP in current 
prices of the corresponding 
period of the previous year. 
 The GDP deflator index characterizes 
the average change in prices in the economy 
and is calculated as the ratio of nominal GDP 
(GDP of the reporting period at current prices) 
to real GDP. 
 
 
 GDP is one of the most important 
indicators of the System of National Accounts, 
which characterizes the final result of the 
production activity of resident economic units, 
which is measured by the value of goods and 
services produced by these units for final use. 
At the same time, the GDP deflator 
index increased to 115.2% after 112.5% in 
January-June 2021. In the same period of 
2020, the GDP deflator index amounted to 
113.6%.
